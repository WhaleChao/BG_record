# Fasting, NPH & Liver — Case Lesson 2026-09-17

*Source: bloodsugar.md 2026-09-16/17 · framework: Dr. Bernstein*
*Case: 14:04 87 + 14:11 4I (fasting) → 17:07 44 (−43/3h)*

## 1. Fasting insulin need is near-zero — liver covers it weakly

- Gap drift 11:59 71 → 14:04 87 = +8/h, fading (post-40 glycogen nearly empty).
- Max ~+24 over 3h *if* sustained — it wasn't; the rise fades on its own.
- Any NPH without food overwhelms this 2:1. Food is what makes basal survivable.

## 2. NPH duration (compressed 4–7h, not textbook 12–18h)

- Morning 06:22 9I done by ~13:30 (7h20m) — confirmed again.
- 14:11 4I: onset 1–2h, peak 2–4h (16:00–18:00), tail to ~19:00–21:00.
- 18:52 5I stacks on 14:11 tail → overlapping peaks 20:00–23:00 (hypo-watch 20:30/21:30).

## 3. Why 4I didn't "hold 80" — non-linear, not subtraction

- NPH both drives peripheral uptake AND suppresses the gluconeogenesis making the rise.
- Once it kicks in, the +8/h stops too → net −30 to −45, not (+24 − 28).
- Post-severe-hypo sensitization (52→40 previous night, 71 morning low, 11h no food): same 4I ≈ −25 replete vs −43 depleted. HAAF + empty liver = no defense.

## 4. Gap rule — BG rising but no meal

| State (fasting, rising) | Action |
|---|---|
| <90 | Eat first, 0I. Recheck. |
| 90–110, rising | Half-dose only (2–4I). |
| ≥110 + food, or sustained rise >15/h | Full dose (6–8I) with meal. |
| Post-hypo <24h | Halve everything above (see §7 why). |

## 6. One-line rule

> Basal without food has no safety buffer. Dose for peak (−40), not onset; rising <90 fasting = eat, never full NPH.

## 7. Why post-hypo <24h halves every dose — yes, hypo sensitizes you

Three mechanisms, all active today:

1. **Glycogen empty:** 52→40 night + 11h no food drained the liver. Normal liver resists ~25pts of a 4I peak; empty liver resists ~0. Same 4I: −25 replete vs −43 today.
2. **HAAF (blunted counterregulation):** each severe hypo (<54) dulls the next epinephrine/glucagon response for 24–48h. Symptoms vanish first ("feeling fine" at 61/71), then the hormone surge itself shrinks — no rescue push when dropping.
3. **Higher peripheral sensitivity:** post-hypo muscle/adipose take up glucose more readily for ~24h (insulin + contraction-independent pathways primed). Basal + even tiny bolus hits harder.

Two-phase trap: first 1–2h looks *resistant* (adrenaline + liver dump → rebound 40→121), tempting a big correction — then phase 2 (2–24h) turns *sensitive* and that correction lands as the next hypo. Hence: let the rebound happen, correct small and awake, halve all fasting basal.
